# Glossary

Plain-language definitions of cybersecurity terms as I learn them.

**Adversarial artificial intelligence (AI)** - A technique that manipulates 
AI and machine learning (ML) technology to conduct attacks more efficiently.

**Advanced persistent threat (APT)** - A threat actor with significant 
expertise who accesses a network without authorization, often researching 
targets in advance and staying undetected long-term. Goals include 
damaging infrastructure or stealing intellectual property.

**Authorized (ethical) hacker** - Follows a code of ethics and the law to 
conduct risk evaluations; motivated to protect people/organizations.

**Business Email Compromise (BEC)** - A type of phishing attack where a 
threat actor impersonates a known source to obtain financial advantage.

**CIA triad** - A model (confidentiality, integrity, availability) that 
helps organizations consider risk when setting up systems and security 
policies.

**CIS (Center for Internet Security)** - A nonprofit providing controls 
to safeguard systems/networks against attacks and guide incident response.

**CISSP** - Certified Information Systems Security Professional; a 
globally recognized, highly sought-after information security 
certification awarded by (ISC)².

**Cloud security** - The process of ensuring that assets stored in the 
cloud are properly configured and access is limited to authorized users.

**Compliance** - The process of adhering to internal standards and external 
regulations. Helps organizations avoid fines and security breaches.

**Computer virus** - Malicious code written to interfere with computer 
operations and cause damage to data and software.

**Cryptographic attack** - An attack that affects secure forms of 
communication between a sender and intended recipient.

**Cybersecurity (security)** - The practice of ensuring confidentiality, 
integrity, and availability of information by protecting networks, devices, 
people, and data from unauthorized access or criminal exploitation.

**FedRAMP** - A U.S. federal program standardizing security assessment, 
authorization, and monitoring of cloud services/products.

**FERC-NERC** - A regulation for organizations working with electricity or 
the U.S./North American power grid; requires preparation for, mitigation 
of, and reporting on security incidents affecting the grid.

**GDPR** - A European Union regulation protecting the data processing and 
privacy rights of E.U. residents; breach notification required within 72 
hours.

**Hacker** - Any person who uses computers to gain access to computer 
systems, networks, or data.

**Hacktivist** - A threat actor driven by a political agenda, using 
digital tech for demonstrations, propaganda, social change, or fame.

**HIPAA** - A 1996 U.S. federal law protecting patients' health 
information; governed by privacy, security, and breach notification rules.

**HITRUST** - A security framework and assurance program that helps 
organizations meet HIPAA compliance.

**Insider threat** - A threat actor who abuses authorized access to obtain 
data that may harm an organization (sabotage, corruption, espionage, 
unauthorized data access/leaks).

**Internal threat** - A current or former employee, external vendor, or 
trusted partner who poses a security risk. Can be accidental or intentional.

**ISO** - International Organization for Standardization; sets 
international standards for technology, manufacturing, and management.

**Malware** - Software designed to harm devices or networks.

**Network security** - The practice of keeping an organization's network 
infrastructure secure from unauthorized access.

**NIST** - The National Institute of Standards and Technology; a U.S. 
agency that develops voluntary compliance frameworks organizations use to 
manage risk.

**NIST CSF** - NIST Cybersecurity Framework.

**NIST RMF** - NIST Risk Management Framework.

**Password attack** - An attempt to access password-secured devices, 
systems, networks, or data.

**PCI DSS** - An international standard ensuring organizations that store/
process/transmit credit card data do so securely, to reduce fraud.

**Personally identifiable information (PII)** - Any information used to 
infer an individual's identity.

**PHI (Protected Health Information)** - Information about an individual's 
past, present, or future physical/mental health, care plans, or payments.

**Phishing** - The use of digital communications to trick people into 
revealing sensitive data or deploying malicious software.

**Physical attack** - A security incident that affects not only digital 
but also physical environments where the incident is deployed.

**Physical social engineering** - An attack in which a threat actor 
impersonates an employee, customer, or vendor to obtain unauthorized 
access to a physical location.

**Programming** - Creating instructions for a computer to execute tasks, 
such as automating repetitive tasks, reviewing web traffic, and alerting 
on suspicious activity.

**Ransomware** - A malicious attack where threat actors encrypt an 
organization's data and demand payment to restore access.

**Security controls** - Safeguards designed to reduce specific security 
risks. Used alongside security frameworks to build a strong security 
posture.

**Security frameworks** - Guidelines used for building plans to help 
mitigate risks and threats to data and privacy.

**Security posture** - An organization's ability to manage its defense of 
critical assets/data and react to change. A strong posture = lower risk.

**Semi-authorized hacker** - A researcher who searches for vulnerabilities 
but doesn't exploit what they find.

**Sensitive personally identifiable information (SPII)** - A specific type 
of PII that falls under stricter handling guidelines.

**Smishing** - The use of text messages to trick users into giving up 
sensitive information or to impersonate a known source.

**SOC (System and Organization Controls) - Type 1, Type 2** - Reports 
(developed by AICPA) assessing an organization's user access policies, 
financial compliance, and risk levels across confidentiality, privacy, 
integrity, availability, and security.

**Social engineering** - A manipulation technique that exploits human 
error to gain private information, access, or valuables.

**Social media phishing** - A type of attack where a threat actor collects 
detailed information about their target on social media sites before 
initiating the attack.

**Spear phishing** - A malicious email attack targeting a specific user or 
group of users, appearing to originate from a trusted source.

**Spyware** - Malware used to gather and sell information without consent 
(emails, texts, recordings, locations, etc.).

**Supply-chain attack** - An attack that targets systems, applications, 
hardware, and/or software to locate a vulnerability where malware can be 
deployed.

**Technical skills** - Skills that require knowledge of specific tools, 
procedures, and policies.

**Threat** - Any circumstance or event that can negatively impact assets.

**Threat actor** - Any person or group who presents a security risk.

**Transferable skills** - Skills from other areas that can apply to 
different careers.

**Unauthorized (unethical) hacker** - A malicious threat actor who 
disregards the law; goal is to collect/sell confidential data for 
financial gain.

**USB baiting** - An attack in which a threat actor strategically leaves a 
malware USB stick for an employee to find and install to unknowingly 
infect a network.

**Virus** - refer to "Computer virus"

**Vishing** - The exploitation of electronic voice communication to 
obtain sensitive information or to impersonate a known source.

**Watering hole attack** - A type of attack when a threat actor 
compromises a website frequently visited by a specific group of users.

**Whaling** - A form of spear phishing targeting company executives to 
gain access to sensitive data.

**Worm** - Malware that duplicates and spreads itself across systems 
without needing to be downloaded by a user.

## Social Engineering Principles (why it works)
- **Authority** - impersonating people with power
- **Intimidation** - bullying/persuading victims
- **Consensus/Social proof** - implying "everyone else did it"
- **Scarcity** - implying limited supply
- **Familiarity** - faking an emotional connection
- **Trust** - building a fake relationship over time
- **Urgency** - pushing quick, unquestioning responses
